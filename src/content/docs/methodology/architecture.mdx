The proposed system integrates dynamic malware analysis, behavioral signature extraction, attack–defense mapping, signature impact quantification, LLM-based residual-severity estimation, and evolutionary optimization into a unified workflow. Each component performs a specific function and provides structured information to the subsequent stage.

The objective of the architecture is to transform observed malware behavior into a set of candidate defensive mechanisms, evaluate the estimated residual severity associated with those mechanisms, and identify a promising defense combination through Cooperative Co-Evolutionary Algorithm (CCEA)-based optimization.

The system consists of an attack dataset, a mapped-defense dataset, a CCEA optimization module, and an external LLM-based residual-severity evaluation module.

The attack and defense datasets provide the candidate inputs to the optimization process. Within the CCEA module, selection and variation generate candidate defense combinations. Each candidate is evaluated using signature impact information and residual-severity estimates obtained from the LLM-based evaluator. The fitness evaluator calculates the fitness of the candidate combination, and the comparator retains promising candidates across generations. The search process produces the recommended defense strategy according to the implemented optimization criteria.

### Architecture Flow

1. **Attack Dataset:** Supplies malware samples and associated behavioral-signature information.
2. **Population of Malware Samples:** Organizes the attack-side candidates used by the optimization process.
3. **Defense Dataset:** Supplies candidate defensive mechanisms and their mappings to observed attack behaviors.
4. **Population of Mapped Defenses:** Organizes the candidate defenses available for selection.
5. **CCEA Optimization Module:** Generates candidate defense combinations through selection and variation.
6. **Impact/Fitness Evaluation:** Evaluates candidate combinations using attack-impact information and residual-severity estimates.
7. **LLM Residual-Severity Module:** Receives the signature–defense combination and relevant reference information and returns a residual-severity estimate.
8. **Comparator and Selection:** Compares candidate fitness values across generations and retains the best candidate found by the search.
9. **Output:** Produces the recommended defense strategy for the evaluated attack scenario.

The LLM-based evaluation module is shown separately because it provides residual-severity estimates to the optimization process. This separation allows the severity-estimation component and the defense-selection component to be evaluated independently.

## System Workflow

The system workflow consists of the following seven consecutive stages:

### Stage 1: Dynamic Malware Analysis
Malware samples are submitted to a controlled Cuckoo Sandbox environment. During execution, the sandbox records behavioral evidence, including triggered signatures and relevant execution events. The resulting reports provide the inputs for subsequent signature extraction and analysis.

### Stage 2: Behavioral Signature Extraction
The generated analysis reports are processed to retrieve the triggered signatures and their relevant attributes. These may include signature names, severity information, API-related behavior, and other available evidence. The extracted information provides a structured representation of the observed attack behaviors.

### Stage 3: ATT&CK Technique Mapping
The observed behaviors are associated with relevant MITRE ATT&CK tactics, techniques, and sub-techniques. This step provides a standardized representation of the adversarial behavior identified during the sandbox analysis.

### Stage 4: Defense Mapping
Candidate defensive mechanisms are identified through the project's ATT&CK–D3FEND mapping process. The resulting defense dataset organizes the defensive techniques associated with the observed attack behaviors and supplies candidate defenses for the optimization stage.

A mapped defense is treated as a candidate for evaluation rather than proof that the attack will be completely mitigated.

### Stage 5: Signature Impact Quantification
The signature impact quantification component assigns initial impact scores and refines them using signature co-occurrence and mutual-information-based information, as described in the project's methodology. These scores provide severity-related inputs for the subsequent attack–defense evaluation and optimization process.

### Stage 6: LLM-Based Residual-Severity Estimation
For each candidate signature–defense combination, the system supplies the LLM-based evaluator with the relevant signature information, defensive mechanism, and associated reference data. The model assesses the potential effect of the defense on the observed behavior and returns a residual-severity (RS) estimate.

The RS estimate represents the severity expected to remain after considering the defensive mechanism. It is an estimated value and must be validated against the project's severity constraints and evaluation data.

### Stage 7: CCEA-Based Defense Optimization
CCEA uses candidate defense combinations generated through selection and variation. The impact/fitness evaluator obtains the RS estimates required for the candidate and calculates its fitness using the implemented fitness function. The comparator retains the best candidate found so far and determines whether further generations should be evaluated.

The process continues until the configured stopping condition is satisfied. The resulting output is the recommended defense combination identified by the search.

## Component Descriptions

### 1. Attack Dataset and Malware-Sample Population
The attack dataset contains the collected malware samples and associated analysis information. After sandbox execution, triggered signatures and relevant severity attributes are extracted to construct the attack representation used by subsequent modules.

The malware-sample population organizes the attack-side information required by the optimization process. The exact representation depends on the sample and signature attributes available in the dataset.

### 2. Defense Dataset and Mapped-Defense Population
The defense dataset stores candidate defensive mechanisms and the mappings established between attack behaviors and defenses. The mapped-defense population provides the candidate defensive techniques that can be considered when constructing a defense combination.

The optimization process is constrained by the candidate defense pool. It cannot select a defensive mechanism that has not been included in the available dataset.

### 3. Selection and Variation
The selection component chooses candidate defenses using information from previous evaluations. The variation component introduces alternatives through the configured exploration and mutation operations.

These operations allow the algorithm to evaluate new combinations while retaining information about previously promising candidates. The process is repeated across generations to improve the fitness of the selected solution.

### 4. LLM-Based Residual-Severity Evaluator
The LLM-based evaluator assesses the relationship between an observed attack behavior and a candidate defense. It receives a signature–defense combination and relevant reference data, then returns an RS estimate for use by the optimization module.

The evaluator and CCEA perform separate tasks: the LLM estimates the residual severity of an attack–defense pair, while CCEA uses the resulting estimates to compare defense combinations.

### 5. Impact/Fitness Evaluator
The impact/fitness evaluator combines the relevant attack-impact scores with the residual-severity values associated with a candidate defense combination. It calculates the combination's fitness according to the project's implemented fitness function.

The resulting score provides a common basis for comparing candidate combinations. The precise calculation, normalization constants, and constraints must be documented alongside the implemented fitness function.

### 6. Comparator and Stopping Mechanism
The comparator evaluates the current candidate against the best solution retained from previous generations. If the current candidate meets the configured improvement criterion, the stored best solution is updated. Otherwise, the algorithm continues its search until the stopping condition is satisfied.

The output is the best candidate identified under the selected initialization, parameters, fitness function, and stopping criteria. It should not be interpreted as a guarantee of a globally optimal defense strategy.

### Residual-Severity Evaluation Data

The LLM evaluation module uses the following conceptual inputs:
- Signature identifier or name.
- Relevant ATT&CK tactic and technique information.
- Initial severity or impact information.
- Candidate defense identifier or name.
- Available structured defense information.
- Other reference data required by the configured evaluation prompt.

Its primary output is the residual-severity estimate. Supporting information, such as the rationale and remaining attack capability, may also be recorded when supported by the configured model output.

The model response must be validated before it is used by the optimization module. The accepted RS value must satisfy the project's defined severity constraints.

### Optimization Output

The final optimization output identifies the selected defense combination and its associated fitness value. Supporting output may include the candidate defense choices, the severity values used during evaluation, the fitness progression, and the stopping condition.

These details help researchers inspect the optimization result and understand how it was selected.

## Architecture Boundaries and Assumptions

The architecture separates observed attack behavior, mapped defenses, residual-severity evaluation, and defense optimization. This separation makes it possible to investigate errors at individual stages instead of treating the entire workflow as a single process.

The system nevertheless depends on the quality of the sandbox observations, the accuracy of signature extraction and attack–defense mappings, the reliability of the LLM's RS estimates, and the fitness function used by CCEA. The architecture does not guarantee that every malware behavior will be observed or that the recommended defense combination will prevent every possible attack.

The output should therefore be interpreted as a recommended defense strategy for the evaluated attack scenario and configured candidate space.
